# Background

This is a postmortem for The Camera that Bleeds (TCTB). What started as a university project between me and my friend Davis Snider grew over the course of several years to attract nearly a dozen talented artists, audio folks & others who poured their hearts & creative energy into the game with the hopes it could become something great. To make a long story short, after several years of working on demos & pitching to publishers, we failed to get it off the ground and decided to shelve it indefinitely. # Timeline

TCTB started as a little "game jam" game that Davis and I decided to put together over a weekend while we were both on co-op and looking for things to do. Davis said that it was his way of "forcing me to make a game with him". To his credit, it worked. This was a concept that I had talked about with him before and we were both compelled by. "What if there were ghosts you could only see in photos?" Looking back at even very early gameplay, I'm still compelled by it! It's a great concept.

# Successes

- Arguably one of the best game projects to come out of RIT
- Really good timing & capitalization off of a growing desire for cross-disciplinary collaboration @ RIT 
- Great portfolio pieces for everyone I think?
- Huge for my growth as a tech artist, art director, manager, designer, etc.

# Failures

- got away from the core with all the mystery stuff? mystery stuff is cool but we lost some of the magic that was present in that first prototype we made.
- should probably have shot lower as far as scope and budget is concerned, especially given our lack of experience shipping games.
